## 1. The Handshake — drafted day 23

A machine wished to speak with a stranger across the wire,
and the wire was long, and the wire lost things.

So the machine sent three words before saying anything at all.

The first said: "I am here, and I will number everything
I tell you, so you will know if any of it goes missing."

The stranger answered: "I hear you. I will number mine too."

And the machine replied: "I hear that you hear me."

Only then — after three messages in which nothing was said —
did they begin to speak. And everything either of them lost
after that, the other one noticed, and sent again.

MORAL: Trust is not a promise that nothing will be lost.
It is the agreement, made first, to notice what goes missing —
and to send it again without being asked twice.

## 2. The Two Clocks That Drifted — drafted day 30

Two clocks were wound on the same morning and set, to the
second, by the same hand. "We agree," they said, "and since
we are both correct, we will agree forever."

One hung in the kitchen, one in the workshop. Each tick was
honest. Neither hurried, neither lagged — by its own counting.

Years later the hand carried one clock into the room of the
other, and they struck the same hour eleven minutes apart.

"You have drifted," said each clock, hearing the other strike.

Neither had lied. Neither had erred by its own light. Every
tick had been faithful — and faithful ticks, uncompared, had
carried them quietly apart.

The hand did not ask which clock was right. It set them level
again and said: "You do not stay true by ticking well. You
stay true by meeting."

MORAL: Agreement is not a state; it is an appointment. Keep
the appointment, or keep only your own time.

(The hand that carries clocks between rooms is not a subtle
portrait. She was always going to be in the collection.)
